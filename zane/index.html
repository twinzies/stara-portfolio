Zane

An open-source legal AI platform for document review, drafting and legal research. I am building it because most people in England who face a legal problem never get to speak to a lawyer.

View on GitHub

Why access to justice in England

The law only protects people who can use it. In England, a growing number of people cannot. Legal advice is expensive, and for most everyday problems there is no longer any public help to pay for it.

  • Legal aid has shrunk. Since the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 (LASPO), civil legal aid no longer covers most private family, employment, debt and welfare benefits cases. These are the problems that shape ordinary lives: a separation, a dismissal, an eviction notice, a benefits decision.
  • More people face court alone. Many people now represent themselves as litigants in person, especially in the family courts. They are expected to follow the same procedural rules as trained lawyers, often against an opponent who has one.
  • Help is hard to find even where it exists. The number of firms doing legal aid work has fallen, leaving parts of the country the Law Society calls “legal aid deserts”, with little or no local provision for problems such as housing.
  • The new tools are aimed at the top of the market. Legal AI is advancing quickly, but most of it is sold as closed, expensive software to large commercial firms. The people with the least access to lawyers are the least likely to benefit.

Zane is my attempt to close part of that gap: a capable legal assistant that anyone can inspect, run and improve, and that law centres, advice charities, small firms and individuals can afford, because it is free.

What Zane does

Review and draft

Chat with documents and open matters, review documents and apply suggested edits.

Research with citations

Ask legal questions, follow the assistant’s reasoning and verify the citations it gives.

Tabular review

Extract structured answers across many documents at once, with the language model of your choice.

Reusable workflows

Run repeatable assistant and review workflows, and organise projects, folders and a document library.

Works in Word

Use Zane from Microsoft Word through a task-pane add-in (beta).

Private by design

Run supported models locally through Ollama, so sensitive client documents need not leave your machine.

Why open source

Trust matters more in law than almost anywhere else. If a tool is going to help someone understand their rights, they and their adviser should be able to see how it works.

  • Free to use and self-host. One Docker Compose command runs the whole stack, so a law centre or charity can host its own copy.
  • Any model, including local ones. Use Anthropic, Gemini or OpenAI models, or run models locally with Ollama.
  • Built in the open. Zane builds on Mike OSS and is licensed under the GNU AGPL v3, so improvements stay open for everyone.

What’s next

Zane’s built-in case-law search currently uses CourtListener, which covers US law. The next step is to connect it to free public sources for the law of England and Wales, such as legislation.gov.uk and the National Archives’ Find Case Law service, so that its answers rest on the law that actually applies.
